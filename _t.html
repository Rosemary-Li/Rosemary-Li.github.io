Research

A Triangulate-and-Audit Framework for Measuring Depression-Indicative Self-Disclosure in YouTube Comments

accepted

Rosemary Li, Nuo Chen, Shenghan Gao, Zhihan Jiang
WellComp @ UbiComp/ISWC 2026, Shanghai

Triangulated a lexical–syntactic rule, SBERT + HDBSCAN clustering, and two LLM annotators over 10,867 comments across 222 videos. Found strong inter-LLM agreement (κ = 0.87) and far higher disclosure in mental-health than music content (OR = 19.1).

nlp / mental health / llm annotation / computational social science

Can LLM Agents Maintain Evidence-Grounded, Point-in-Time Credit States Across Corporate Disclosures?

ongoing

Rosemary Li, with incoming Columbia EE faculty

A benchmark for whether LLM agents can carry a company’s credit state — capital structure, liquidity, credit metrics, maturity wall, material change — across successive disclosures, grounding every claim in the filing that supports it and respecting what was knowable at each point in time. Built on a credit-specific ontology rather than generic financial QA, with evidence annotation under practitioner review, point-in-time data boundaries and temporal-leakage tests, and persistent agent memory as an ablation variable. First stage of a four-part program: benchmark → credit state model → credit research agent → multi-agent credit market simulation.

llm agents / benchmark design / credit analysis / evidence grounding / evaluation

LLM Agent Economies and Market Simulation

ongoing

Rosemary Li, with incoming Columbia EE faculty

Investigating whether LLM-agent market simulators reproduce real-world phenomena. Replicating market-based agent-evolution frameworks; identified a birth-luck variance evaluation gap in auction, rent, and bankruptcy dynamics over prompt populations. Existing equity simulators evolve agents in prompt space alone, without endogenous price formation or strategic behaviour such as spoofing and collusion — the gap that motivates the credit-side work above.

llm agents / market simulation / evaluation
